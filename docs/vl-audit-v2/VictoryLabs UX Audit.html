UX / UI AUDIT · INTERNAL · FOR DESIGN + ENG REVIEW

A terminal wants to disappear.
Right now yours is asking to be looked at.

VictoryLabs reads as a premium dark product — the type ramp, the violet accent, the muted chrome are all working. But four issues compound across every screen: content is centered into ~50% of the viewport on a desktop-first product; color is overloaded (green/red carry direction and status and identity); badges multiply next to primary text and steal scan-time from prices and timestamps; and row density is comfortable, not tight — a feed that updates 150 rows/s should feel like a tape, not a list. Fixing four to six tokens-level decisions removes most of the noise without changing your identity.

§ 01
Executive summary

What this product is vs. what it currently looks like.

Reading the four screens as a stranger, I would describe VictoryLabs as "a premium NFT analytics tool that aspires to be a terminal but is currently presented as a content site." The aspiration is correct. The execution is being held back by layout cadence, color semantics, and a few small but compounding density choices. None of this is identity-level work.

The diagnosis

1. Viewport utilization. Every screen renders the working table inside a narrow centered card with very large left/right gutters. On a 27" monitor that is the single most expensive thing in the design — you are paying for screen real estate you are not using. Real terminals fill edge-to-edge with rule-based gutters, not cards.

2. Color is doing too many jobs. Green means buy, active, positive delta, watch state, and live indicator simultaneously. The same is true for red, and amber leaks between "warning" and "timestamp." The eye cannot pre-attentively distinguish status from value.

3. Badge density. Row headers carry up to five small pills (WATCH, ME, ↑, X, LMNFT) before you reach a number. A trader scans numbers; pills should be secondary.

4. Row height is comfortable, not tight. Avatars run ~54–80px tall in the Feed, which forces a comfy line-height and limits visible rows to ~7. A tape should show 25–40.

§ 02 · IMPACT/HIGH
Must fix

High-impact: scan speed and density.

Each of these is the kind of finding a senior trader would call out in 30 seconds of using the product. They are mostly token-level or component-level changes — not redesigns — and they unlock everything downstream.
H-01 Kill the centered card. Let the working surface fill the viewport. High impact Complexity · Medium
Why it matters
On every screen, the table sits inside a rounded panel with ~25–30% empty space on each side. On a 1440–1920px display this means a trader sees ~7 feed rows where they could see 18–25. Width is the cheapest density lever you have.
UX principle
Information density > chrome. Terminals (Bloomberg, IB TWS, OptionsHouse, Hyperliquid) anchor data to the viewport edges and use thin internal rules — not cards — to separate regions. The card aesthetic is a SaaS convention; it does not belong on a tape.
Risk / tradeoff
Some users like the framed feel. Mitigate by keeping a thin 12–16px outer gutter and a 1px outer rule, but drop the panel background and rounded radius on the main work surface.
Affected
FeedMintsToolsBoard
H-02 Cut row height by 35–45% on the Feed. Shrink avatars from ~54–80px to 24–28px. High impact Complexity · Small
Why it matters
The Live Events feed currently shows ~7 rows above the fold. Each row carries one identifier, one price, one delta — three pieces of information per ~110px of vertical space. Target: ~32–40px per row, 20–30 rows visible.
UX principle
Fitts' law + foveal scan. When events arrive at >1/s, the user is not clicking individual rows — they are pattern-matching streams (clusters, repeats, outliers). Pattern-match requires many rows in the foveal cone simultaneously.
Risk / tradeoff
Avatars become harder to recognize at 24px. Mitigate: keep the larger avatar in a hover preview / right-rail detail panel. Or — better — expose density as a per-page setting (you already have Tape/Compact/Comfy modes; the Feed defaults to Comfy when it should default to Compact).
Affected
FeedMints sidebar
H-03 Fix color semantics. Green/red is for direction, not for class. High impact Complexity · Small
Why it matters
BUY badges are green, AMM badges are red — but AMM is not "bad," it is a routing class. Meanwhile, +42.8 spread on the Tools page is green (good for buyer, bad for seller — but the page is about seller offers, so green here is reversed). The same colors are also used for delta arrows, live dots, status pills. The result: a glance does not tell you direction.
UX principle
One channel, one meaning. Reserve saturated green/red for signed numeric deltas only. Use a third color family (violet / cyan) for class tags, and use luminance (not hue) for status (active = full, expired = 40% opacity).
Risk / tradeoff
Existing users have learned the current palette. Migrate behind a setting (color v2) and ship with both available for two weeks before flipping default. The change is small CSS but the muscle-memory cost is real.
Concrete swaps
BUY → neutral chip on white text · AMM → violet outline · SPREAD → invert sign-color on offers page (negative = good for seller) · LIVE/ACTIVE dot → keep green, but desaturate the timestamp pink/amber.
H-04 Stop coloring timestamps. "14s ago" should not be pink. High impact Complexity · Trivial
Why it matters
On the Feed, timestamps render in a salmon/pink that reads as a warning state. On the Mints sidebar, they render in amber — which reads as caution. Neither is what the data means; both pull foveal attention away from the price.
UX principle
Don't paint metadata. Timestamps are reference, not signal. They belong at ~50–60% luminance on the neutral ramp, never on a hue axis. Use color only when a timestamp itself crosses a threshold (e.g. <5s ago = subtle green pulse).
Risk / tradeoff
None. This is a one-token change. If you want freshness signaling, layer it as a 200ms opacity flash on row insert instead.
Affected
FeedMints sidebar
H-05 Collapse the badge wall next to collection names. High impact Complexity · Small
Why it matters
On Mints, a row reads: "WATCH · GAQQ · ME · ↑ · X · LMNFT" before you reach the first number. That's six tokens and five different visual treatments competing with the collection name itself. A trader's eye should land on GAQQ, then on the price — not be parsed left-to-right like a sentence.
UX principle
Primary nouns get primary weight. The collection name is the noun. Everything else is a secondary attribute — and secondary attributes belong in the row's right-side actions group, a hover-revealed tray, or a single status dot.
Risk / tradeoff
Watch / active status genuinely needs to be glanceable. Mitigate: encode WATCH/ACTIVE as a single 4px colored left edge (you almost do this already — commit to it and remove the pill). Move ME/X/LMNFT to a right-side icon tray.
Concrete
4 badges → 1 left-edge state · 3 marketplace icons grouped right · collection name +1 weight step.
H-06 Unify the SPREAD column's color logic on the Tools page. High impact Complexity · Trivial
Why it matters
The Tools page is "personal offers received on your listings." A spread of +42.8 means an offer 42% above your listing — that's good for you. But it renders green simply because the number is positive. Meanwhile −36.1% renders red and is in fact a deep lowball. The arithmetic sign is doing the semantic work, but the semantics are reversed.
UX principle
Color should encode meaning, not arithmetic. The user does not care that 42.8 is positive; they care that 42.8 is favorable.
Risk / tradeoff
Inverting feels wrong to anyone who has internalized the current behavior. Ship a small inline tooltip on first hover: "green = favorable to you (offer ≥ listing)". Then never speak of it again.
Affected
Tools / ME offers
H-07 Tabular figures + consistent decimal places for all SOL prices. High impact Complexity · Trivial
Why it matters
On the Feed: 0.080, 0.79, 0.036, 0.014, 1.09. Five different decimal precisions in five rows. The decimal point shifts horizontally row to row, so the user cannot pre-attentively compare magnitudes. The price column should be a tabular figures monospace with right-aligned decimals.
UX principle
Align on the decimal. This is universal in trading UIs and the single highest-leverage change for numeric scanability. Cost: a font-feature-setting and a CSS class.
Risk / tradeoff
You may need 3–4 decimals on sub-0.01 prices and 2 decimals on >1 SOL prices. Pick a single precision per price bucket and right-align on the decimal separator, not the number's right edge.
Concrete
Use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums + a custom decimal-aligned formatter. Apply globally to .price, .spread, .delta.

§ 03 · IMPACT/MED
Polish that compounds

Medium-impact: consistency and interaction language.

These don't change the product's perceived quality on first sight, but they remove the small frictions that make a power-user feel like they're working on the tool instead of through it.
M-01 Unify the row-state language across pages. Medium impact Complexity · Medium
Why it matters
Feed rows have a violet left edge. Mints rows have green/red/yellow left edges. Tools rows have a colored left edge for some rows and not others. Board rows have small red/green edges. The same visual signal means four different things across four pages.
UX principle
One mark, one meaning, everywhere. Pick one semantic for the left edge (recommendation: row freshness / time-decay — full opacity at insert, fading over 30s) and apply it on every list.
Risk / tradeoff
Loses some category-coding on Mints. Replace with a small classification icon at the row's left, after the edge.
Affected
FeedMintsToolsBoard
M-02 Replace the "—" coef/avg placeholder with a dim numeric zero or empty cell. Medium impact Complexity · Trivial
Why it matters
"—" reads as "this thing is here, but missing" and creates visual weight every time. In a column of mostly-empty values, that weight stacks into a vertical wall.
UX principle
Absence should be quiet. Either render nothing (true empty) or render a 25% opacity ·. Reserve "—" for cases where the value is meaningfully not-applicable versus simply not-yet-computed.
Risk / tradeoff
None substantive. Test with a column-toggle so users who want the placeholder back can opt in.
Affected
MintsBoard
M-03 Standardize sparkline color: only two hues, mapped to 7d-delta sign. Medium impact Complexity · Trivial
Why it matters
On Board, sparklines render in blue, green, red, and purple — apparently per-collection identity. This is not signal. It is decoration that competes with the actual signal (the slope of the line).
UX principle
Color where there's no other channel. A sparkline already encodes change via slope and shape. Use color only to encode the sign of the 7d delta. One green hue, one red hue, neutral otherwise.
Risk / tradeoff
Loses per-row "color-coding" which some users may have grown attached to. Replace with a single 2px colored tick at the line's end-cap if you need a per-row identifier.
Affected
Board
M-04 Make hover/focus states visible at terminal speeds. Medium impact Complexity · Small
Why it matters
The screenshots don't show hover, but inferring from common dark-terminal patterns: hover is probably a 4% background lift. On a fast-scrolling tape that's invisible. A trader needs to know where the row will commit before clicking.
UX principle
Hover is feedback, not flair. Use a 1px violet left-rule appearing on hover (already part of your vocabulary) + an 8% background lift + a subtle 1px outer outline at the row's right-edge action group.
Risk / tradeoff
Hover-flash on rapidly-arriving rows can feel jittery. Disable hover-lift while the row is <1s old and the feed is unpaused.
Affected
All
M-05 Animation budget: insert and price-flash only. Nothing else. Medium impact Complexity · Small
Why it matters
Subtle is your stated philosophy and the screenshots back this — but on a 150-row tape, even one unnecessary transition compounds. Codify it: row-insert uses a 120ms opacity+y-translate; price flash uses a 180ms background pulse; everything else is instantaneous.
UX principle
Perceived responsiveness = first paint < 100ms. Transitions on filter changes, mode switches, tab toggles should not animate. They feel slow even at 150ms.
Risk / tradeoff
Some surfaces (Tools page mode switches) genuinely benefit from a 100ms crossfade. Allow it, but cap at 120ms.
Affected
All
M-06 "ME OK" / "MINTS ON" / "OFF" status chips: too many, too prominent. Medium impact Complexity · Small
Why it matters
The top bar reads "MODE: SALES ONLY · MINTS ON · OFF" — three controls in three different visual treatments (label-value, outlined chip, outlined chip in red). The hierarchy implies they're peers; the colors imply they're not.
UX principle
Toggle visual = toggle behavior. Mode switches that toggle the same kind of state should look identical. Either all three are pill toggles, or all three are dropdowns.
Risk / tradeoff
OFF is currently red to signal "kill switch" — that semantic is worth keeping, but should be a single iconic kill button (square, red outline) clearly separated from the toggles.
Affected
Global header
M-07 Tools page: dim EXPIRED rows to ~40% opacity. Don't show price/spread in full. Medium impact Complexity · Trivial
Why it matters
EXPIRED rows currently show full-weight prices, full-color spreads (+1973.7%, +600%, +468%) — they compete with the 3 ACTIVE rows above for attention. The eye is drawn to the largest number, which is a dead offer.
UX principle
State drives weight. Inactive data should be ~40–50% opacity by default, with a hover-restore to full opacity for inspection.
Risk / tradeoff
None. Users who care about expired data can still see it; users who don't aren't distracted.
Affected
Tools
M-08 Page titles ("Live events", "Live mint tracker", "Trending collections") are typographically weak. Medium impact Complexity · Trivial
Why it matters
Each page introduces itself with a 20–22px sentence-case label. Compare to the typographic weight of the data below it — the title is doing less work than a single row. A terminal doesn't need a page title at all if the nav already tells you where you are.
UX principle
If the nav says it, the page shouldn't repeat it. Option A: drop the title entirely; show only the live-state meta line. Option B: use the Instrument Serif italic at 14–16px as a refined byline ("live events").
Risk / tradeoff
Removing a title can feel disorienting on first load. Keep it but cap at 16px and pair with the count + live-dot inline.
Affected
All
M-09 Footer / status bar: 8 chips, no hierarchy. Trader's most-watched numbers are buried. Medium impact Complexity · Small
Why it matters
The footer reads: Discord · Twitter · 0 alerts · FEES · SOUND · TPS 3 538 · SOL $83.61 · live · EVENTS 100. These are six classes of data given one weight. SOL price + TPS are the two most-watched terminal numbers; they should be biggest. Discord/Twitter are vanity links; smallest.
UX principle
Status bar = market dashboard. Bloomberg's status bar shows: time, latency, P&L, market state. Yours has the right ingredients; they need ranking.
Risk / tradeoff
Rebalancing might break muscle memory for power users. Ship behind a v2 toggle.
Affected
Global footer
§ 04 · IMPACT/LOW
Nice polish

Low-impact: craftsmanship.

Nothing here is a problem. These are the moves that take a B+ product to an A — the kinds of details that traders notice subconsciously and that elevate perceived quality without you having to point at them.
L-01Address truncation: switch from "F9Qh…WMJu" to "F9Qh→WMJu" or middle-ellipsis with monospace.LowTrivial
Why
The ellipsis "…" is visually heavier than the surrounding glyphs in most monospaced fonts. A right-arrow or a narrow ellipsis (U+2026 in a tighter font, or a 2-dot leader) reads cleaner at small sizes.
Risk
None. Pure type detail.
L-02Use Instrument Serif italic for a single editorial accent per page — not for the brand mark and nothing else.LowTrivial
Why
Right now the serif italic lives in the logo and nowhere else. It's a beautiful asset earning its keep in one place. Apply it sparingly: section labels ("live", "trending") or empty-state copy.
Risk
Overuse defeats the rarity. Cap at one italic phrase per page.
L-03Search bar is 50% of viewport width and visually heavier than the nav. Shrink to ~28% and right-align.LowTrivial
Why
Search is a secondary action on a tape — primary use is observation, not discovery. The "/" keyboard shortcut indicator suggests power users use the hotkey, so the visual weight is wasted.
Risk
Some users discover collections via search-as-you-type. Keep that working; just shrink the field.
L-04Number formatting: use a thin space (U+202F) as thousand-separator, not a regular space.LowTrivial
Why
"11 776" and "3 538" currently use a full space. At tight row heights the gap looks like a column break. A thin or hair space is unambiguous.
Risk
None.
L-05Top-right MODE/MINTS/OFF cluster: add a 1px vertical rule between MODE and the toggles.LowTrivial
Why
Mode (read-only state) and toggles (interactive) are different classes. A 1px rule groups them appropriately without adding chrome.
Risk
None.
L-06PC / LAPTOP / PHONE density switcher: relocate to a "density" menu inside settings instead of always-on bottom right.LowSmall
Why
Power users set density once. After that the switcher is always-on chrome with no recurring value. Move to settings; expose via keyboard shortcut.
Risk
Discoverability drops on first visit. Mitigate with a 5-second toast on first session.

§ 06
Fix · Polish · Keep

The cheat sheet.

If you only read one section: this one. Action items split into three buckets — what to fix this sprint, what to polish over time, and what to not touch under any circumstances.
Must fix · this quarter
  • Decimal alignment on every numeric column (tabular-nums + formatter).
  • Compress Feed row height to ~32–40px; default to Compact density.
  • De-color timestamps. Move "14s ago" to the neutral ramp.
  • Fix SPREAD semantics on Tools page (sign is not direction).
  • Collapse badge stack next to collection names.
  • Fill the viewport. Remove the centered card panel on tables.
  • Unify left-edge meaning across Feed/Mints/Tools/Board.
Nice polish · over time
  • Sparkline color → two-hue (sign of 7d delta) only.
  • Dim EXPIRED rows to 40% opacity on Tools page.
  • Animation budget — codify insert (120ms) and price-flash (180ms); nothing else.
  • Hover visibility — 8% lift + 1px violet left-rule on row hover.
  • Address arrows — F9Qh→WMJu instead of stacked seller/buyer lines.
  • Status bar reranking — SOL price + TPS get top weight; social links get bottom.
  • Editorial italic — one Instrument Serif accent per page, not zero, not three.
Do NOT touch
  • The violet accent system. It's distinctive without being Web3-tacky. Don't drift toward cyan, don't drift toward neon.
  • The dark canvas. Resist any "light mode" request. It's wrong for a terminal.
  • The information density philosophy. Stated and observed — don't let SaaS habits pad it back out.
  • Density modes (PC/Laptop/Phone/Tape/Compact/Comfy). Keep them; just fix the default per page.
  • The serif italic VictoryLabs mark. It's the strongest piece of identity on the screen.
  • The card-less Board sparklines and the inline volume bars. Those mini-vis are well-judged.
  • Keyboard shortcuts (the "/" hint). Power-user respect. Add more, never fewer.
  • The "Pause" + "Filters" placement on the Feed. Right-aligned, secondary weight — correct.

Per-page notes

Quick reads, page by page.

A skim layer for the leads who don't read the whole audit. One paragraph per screen.
P-01FeedMost upside—
The hardest-working page in the product and the one currently doing the least with its space. Single biggest win: drop avatars to 24px, halve row padding, kill the centered card, and re-neutralize timestamps. After those four changes the Feed will feel like a different product without changing a single concept.
P-02MintsHigh noise—
The split layout (main table + right sidebar feed) is structurally good — the right rail is doing real work. The badge wall next to each collection name is the headline problem. Once those collapse, the visual hierarchy of MINTS / SUPPLY / LAST MINT / COEF / RATE columns will read clearly. Secondary: the COEF column's "—" placeholders need to recede.
P-03Tools · ME personal offersSemantic bug—
Color semantics on SPREAD are the loudest issue — the column is currently more confusing than helpful. After flipping, dim the 5+ EXPIRED rows. The page then becomes a tight 3-row actionable list with historical context dimmed below, which is exactly what an offers-management tool should be.
P-04Board · trending collectionsClosest to right—
The most polished page in the audit. The combination of rank-number, avatar, name, and inline sparkline + bar-chart is doing real work and shouldn't be touched structurally. Only two changes needed: collapse sparkline color to two hues (sign of 7d delta), and clarify what the SALES column's red vs. green encoding actually means — right now it reads as ambiguous (volume threshold? momentum? direction?).
§ 08
Closing

The honest read.

VictoryLabs is good. The instincts behind it — dense, dark, violet accent, restrained chrome — are correct, and most of the design vocabulary is already in place. What's holding it back is not vision but discipline at the token layer: a half-dozen decisions about color semantics, row height, and viewport utilization are being made differently on every page. Codify those at the tokens level, ship the seven Must-Fix items, and the perceived quality will jump a full grade without the user being able to explain why. The Top-5 ROI list is the place to start. Nothing else in this document matters until those are done.
A note on what was deliberately not recommended. No new charts. No new widgets. No accessibility-compliance boilerplate. No glassmorphism. No light theme. No mobile-first padding. No SaaS hero sections. No identity changes. No removal of density. This is on purpose: a trading terminal's job is to disappear into the data, and most "redesigns" make the chrome more visible, not less.
